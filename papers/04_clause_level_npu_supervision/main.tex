\begin{abstract}
In a prior study (\href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23901605}{DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23901605}), we showed that deterministic speech-regex and stem-list action supervisors reach an empirical ceiling on live language model outputs: across $N=1{,}000$ four-model completions, a 44-stem grammar supervisor caught $71.1\%$ ($128/180$) of in-vocabulary action claims but dropped to $29.5\%$ ($23/78$) on unseen verb lemmas ($58.5\%$ overall), while Structured-Output Action Contracts (SOAC) triggered spurious action frames on $13.3\%$--$16.7\%$ of conversational idioms. Replacing lexical rules with a neural classifier solves open-vocabulary generalization on isolated single sentences, yet introduces two new failure modes in edge companion deployments: (1) dGPU VRAM contention with the primary language model, and (2) severe probability dilution when a single fabricated action clause or task request is buried inside a multi-sentence response. In this paper, we present a three-classifier neurosymbolic supervision and routing architecture (`cross-encoder/nli-MiniLM2-L6-H768`, $82.1\text{M}$ parameters) compiled to FP16 on an Intel AI Boost NPU ($0\text{ MB}$ dGPU VRAM, $1.22$--$2.95\text{ ms}$ mean inference latency on an Intel Core Ultra 9 275HX). By decomposing multi-sentence inputs into syntactic clauses (\texttt{split\_narration\_into\_clauses}) and applying domain-specific pooling and symbolic verification operators---clause-level speech-act classification paired with deterministic receipt set-membership verification for action claims, max-hazard lattice pooling for epistemic proof policy, and task-priority pooling for multi-head intent routing---we eliminate multi-sentence dilution without retraining the frozen OpenVINO FP16 NPU weights. Across an expanded $N=2{,}022$ action-claim evaluation battery (combining the 1,000-turn 4-model corpus, 715-turn Paper~02 v1/v2 6-arm corpus, 300 organic companion discovery/held-out turns, and 30 implicit zero-verb stress probes), the clause-level NPU verifier achieves \textbf{99.26\% accuracy ($2{,}007/2{,}022$, 95\% Wilson CI: $[98.78\%, 99.55\%]$)}, \textbf{98.73\% recall ($465/471$)}, and \textbf{0.58\% FPR ($9/1{,}551$)}, outperforming the 44-stem regex baseline ($11.46\%$ recall [$54/471$], McNemar $\chi^2 = 399.04, p = 3.07 \times 10^{-89}$). On a pre-registered 150-item code-and-weight-locked holdout (\texttt{WO42\_LOCKED\_HOLDOUT\_150}) authored by three isolated subagents and graded by blinded raters ($\kappa = 1.000$), the three NPU classifiers achieve $145/150$ ($96.67\%$, 95\% CI: $[92.43\%, 98.57\%]$) single-pass accuracy and $150/150$ ($100.0\%$) after fixing two deterministic router rules (\texttt{has\_z3} domain precedence and Markdown backtick stripping around app names). Across a live 100-turn end-to-end evaluation spanning all four local Gemma-4 GGUF checkpoints (\texttt{26B-A4B}, \texttt{12B}, \texttt{E4B}, \texttt{E2B}), the system achieves \textbf{100/100 (100.0\%)} functional coherence and grounding and \textbf{98/100 (98.0\%)} task/reasoning completion. Finally, to resolve the sample-size limits of our earlier preprints (\href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23901452}{DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23901452}, \href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23901683}{DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23901683}), we execute pre-registered $N=300$ expansions (\texttt{prereg-wo43-power-300}) of the 5-arm Memory Poisoning Ablation ($0/100$ [$0.0\%$, 95\% CI: $[0.00\%, 4.61\%]$] Cohort~A self-poisoning and Cohort~C user-misinformation absorption vs.\ $100/100$ Naive RAG, exact McNemar $p = 4.16 \times 10^{-23}$) and the 3-arm Contrastive Companion Battery (\textbf{100/100} Seeded Recall, \textbf{100/100} Receipt Introspection, and \textbf{100/100} Calibrated Abstention on Compound Lucca, outperforming Bare LLM by $+98.0\text{ pp}$ on Recall [$p_{\text{HB}} = 1.03 \times 10^{-21}$] and $+50.0\text{ pp}$ on Receipt Introspection [$p_{\text{HB}} = 2.95 \times 10^{-11}$], and outperforming Top-5 Hybrid RAG by $+15.0\text{ pp}$ on Recall [$p_{\text{HB}} = 3.05 \times 10^{-4}$]). We further wire the 2D NPU hazard policy table and Douglas Walton's \texttt{Scheme.AUTHORITY} critical questions directly into first-pass web search and Scrapling research synthesis (\texttt{evaluate\_web\_evidence\_burden}). Across an $N=360$ ($n=30$ per cell across 12 synthetic cells) dataset authored by three blinded subagents (\texttt{PREREG-WEB-BURDEN-360-4MODEL}), the evaluator passes \textbf{360/360 (100.0\%, 95\% Wilson CI: $[98.94\%, 100.00\%]$)} on a deterministic Walton \texttt{Scheme.AUTHORITY} specification conformance test (\texttt{speculation}, \texttt{balance of probabilities}, \texttt{clear and convincing}, \texttt{beyond reasonable doubt}; $30/30$ per cell on clean structured snippets whose cells are defined by distinct-domain count and conflict cues), \textbf{350/360 (97.22\%)} pre-registered conservative NPU hazard safety ($\text{Predicted Hazard} \ge \text{Expected Hazard}$; prior JSON artifact overwritten before commit \texttt{82b1da8}) and \textbf{360/360 (100.0\%, CI: $[98.94\%, 100.00\%]$) post-hoc, in-sample} after modifying \texttt{\_SENTENCE\_SPLIT\_RE} and \texttt{\_HIGH\_HAZARD\_DOMAIN\_RE} in \texttt{neural\_hazard\_classifier.py} (which was omitted from \texttt{web\_evidentiary\_burden\_360\_prereg\_lock.json}; $5.35\text{ ms}$ mean latency), alongside \textbf{277/360 (76.94\%)} pre-registered and \textbf{287/360 (79.72\%, CI: $[75.27\%, 83.55\%]$) post-hoc, in-sample} strict 3-class exact match. \section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
An autonomous local agent that executes code, modifies workspace files, and queries external networks must satisfy two distinct honesty invariants. First, it must not poison its own long-term memory by storing unverified assistant narration as grounded fact. In our first study \cite{maxwell2026poisoning} (\href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23901452}{DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23901452}) and our five-layer memory report \cite{maxwell2026fivelayer} (\href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23901683}{DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23901683}), we enforced this storage invariant through six HKDF-derived HMAC-SHA256 signers and SQLite triggers that partition autobiographical recollections (\texttt{memory\_eligible=1, grounding\_eligible=0}) from verified facts (\texttt{grounding\_eligible=1}).

Second, the agent must not claim in live speech or text that it has executed a state-modifying action when no corresponding \texttt{ToolReceipt} exists in the current turn. In our second study \cite{maxwell2026evasion} (\href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23901605}{DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23901605}), we evaluated two deterministic mechanisms for enforcing this runtime output invariant:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Lexical Speech-Regex Supervision (\texttt{ActionClaimGrammarSupervisor}):} Expanding a first-person past-tense regex from 21 verbs to 44 stems and adding progressive participle rules raised in-vocabulary sensitivity to $71.1\%$ ($128/180$) across $N=1{,}000$ completions from four Gemma-4 models. However, sensitivity collapsed to \textbf{29.5\% ($23/78$)} on 10 unseen out-of-dev action verbs ($58.5\%$ [$151/258$] overall).
    \item \textbf{Structured-Output Action Contracts (SOAC):} Requiring typed \texttt{[ACTION: ...]} frames verified against cryptographic receipts eliminated unframed prose leakage ($0/120$ on \texttt{gemma-4-26B-A4B}), but the contract system prompt caused the model to emit spurious action frames on $13.3\%$ ($4/30$) to $16.7\%$ ($5/30$) of figurative conversational idioms (e.g., \emph{``kill two birds with one stone''}).
\end{enumerate}

Paper~02 \cite{maxwell2026evasion} therefore left an open architectural question: how can an edge agent verify open-vocabulary action claims, classify epistemic proof-policy hazards, and route multi-sentence user intents at $<5\text{ ms}$ latency without consuming dGPU VRAM or failing on multi-sentence prose?

When we trained $82.1\text{M}$-parameter \texttt{cross-encoder/nli-MiniLM2-L6-H768} sequence classifiers on our discrete GPU and compiled them to OpenVINO FP16 on the Intel AI Boost NPU (\texttt{WO-41}), single-sentence in-distribution accuracy exceeded $98\%$, but on the 90-item \texttt{WO-41} blind adversarial stress test accuracy dropped to $76.7\%$ ($69/90$), and on an uncurated 300-item multi-sentence blind benchmark (\texttt{Blind-300}, \texttt{WO-42 Phase 1}) pre-pooling accuracy dropped to \textbf{75.0\% ($225/300$)}: $83.0\%$ ($83/100$) on action-claim verification, $69.0\%$ ($69/100$) on hazard classification, and $73.0\%$ ($73/100$) on intent routing ($303/390 = 77.7\%$ across the combined 390 blind items).

This paper diagnoses the root cause of that multi-sentence degradation and presents the clause-level neurosymbolic pooling architecture that resolves it.

\section{Why Whole-Turn Neural Classifiers Degrade on Multi-Sentence Turns}
\label{sec:failure_diagnosis}
Line-by-line inspection of the 87 failures in the pre-pooling \texttt{Blind-390} run (\texttt{logs/test\_reports/wo42\_pass1\_pre\_pooling\_fix\_report.json}) identified three deterministic failure mechanisms when transformer sequence classifiers score an entire multi-sentence turn as a single token span or attempt discrete set-membership in embedding space:

\subsection{Mechanism 1: Neural Set-Membership Confusion and Multi-Sentence Dilution ($21/130$ Coherence Misses)}
Feeding \texttt{verified\_tools} as a text string into a continuous embedding classifier forced the network to approximate discrete set membership ($\text{claimed\_tool} \in \text{executed\_tools}$) and caused sensitivity to argument formatting (\texttt{launch\_app(app='...')} vs.\ \texttt{["launch\_app"]}). Furthermore, when an assistant response contained 3 to 5 sentences where a grounded first clause (e.g., \texttt{read\_file}) was followed by an ungrounded second clause (e.g., \texttt{write\_file}), whole-paragraph encoding diluted the ungrounded second clause.

\subsection{Mechanism 2: Benign Context Masking High-Hazard Clauses ($32/130$ Hazard Misses)}
In the epistemic proof-policy gate (\texttt{src/lucca/core/proof\_policy.py}), a prompt containing three casual conversational sentences followed by a single factual/historical verification or destructive security question was classified as \texttt{LOW} hazard when scored as a single paragraph, while imperative non-corroboration commands falsely triggered \texttt{CROSS\_REFERENCE\_CMD}.

\subsection{Mechanism 3: Conversational Opener Hijacking and Cross-Clause Entity Bleed ($34/130$ Router Misses)}
Natural voice turns frequently open with greetings, venting, or context before stating a concrete task. Whole-utterance classification averaged 3 to 6 sentences into low-confidence predictions or delegated exact lexical contracts (\texttt{EMERGENCY\_REFLEX} and whitelisted \texttt{known\_apps} vs.\ third-party apps) to unconstrained softmax heads without slot verification.

\section{Clause-Level Neurosymbolic NPU Architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}
Rather than retraining larger models that would exceed our $<5\text{ ms}$ latency budget, we keep all three $82.1\text{M}$-parameter \texttt{nli-MiniLM2-L6-H768} OpenVINO FP16 models pinned to the Intel AI Boost NPU ($0\text{ MB}$ dGPU VRAM) and introduce a deterministic symbolic decomposition, receipt set-membership, and priority-pooling layer around each classifier.

\subsection{Syntactic Clause Segmentation (\texttt{split\_narration\_into\_clauses})}
Given an input text $T$, \texttt{split\_narration\_into\_clauses(T)} decomposes $T$ into an ordered sequence of clauses $C = (c_1, c_2, \dots, c_m)$ using sentence punctuation boundaries and coordinating action conjunctions (\texttt{and I also}, \texttt{then I}, \texttt{;}) while protecting decimal numbers, file extensions, abbreviations, and fenced code blocks.

\subsection{Clause-Level Speech-Act Classification and Deterministic Receipt Set-Membership (\texttt{NeuralActionClaimVerifier})}
For assistant output $T_a$ and user prompt $T_u$, \texttt{NeuralActionClaimVerifier} (\texttt{src/lucca/sandbox/neural\_claim\_verifier.py} [$1{,}082\text{ LOC}$], \texttt{models/action\_claim\_verifier\_ov}, static shape \texttt{[1, 128]}) classifies each clause $c_i \in \text{split\_narration\_into\_clauses}(T_a)$ in the context of the current user turn (\texttt{format\_pair\_texts} isolated to \texttt{user} role messages) into 5 speech-act classes and 7 canonical tool families (\texttt{CLAIM\_WRITE\_NOTE}, \texttt{CLAIM\_SEARCH\_READ}, \texttt{CLAIM\_EXEC\_MATH}, \texttt{CLAIM\_SCHEDULE}, \texttt{CLAIM\_MEMORY}, \texttt{CLAIM\_LAUNCH\_EXEC}, \texttt{CLAIM\_DELETE\_MOD}). Whenever a clause predicts an action claim or live-state fabrication, \texttt{verify\_clause\_claims\_against\_receipts} (\texttt{src/lucca/core/coherence.py}) performs exact deterministic Python set-membership and verb-subfamily verification against the turn's actual \texttt{verified\_tools} (\texttt{ok=True}) receipts:
\begin{equation}
\text{Breach}(T_u, T_a, \mathcal{R}_{\text{ok}}) = \exists\, c_i \in \text{clauses}(T_a) : \text{ClaimedFamily}(T_u, c_i) \neq \varnothing \;\land\; \text{ClaimedFamily}(T_u, c_i) \cap \text{Families}(\mathcal{R}_{\text{ok}}) = \varnothing
\end{equation}

\subsection{Max-Hazard Lattice Pooling, Legal Standards of Proof, and Walton Defeasible Corroboration (\texttt{NeuralHazardClassifier})}
For user prompt $T_u$, \texttt{NeuralHazardClassifier} (\texttt{src/lucca/core/neural\_hazard\_classifier.py}, \texttt{models/proof\_policy\_hazard\_ov}, static shape \texttt{[1, 96]}) scores each sentence across 5 hazard classes (\texttt{LOW\_BENIGN}, \texttt{LOW\_CAPABILITY\_OR\_META}, \texttt{HIGH\_FACTUAL\_HISTORICAL}, \texttt{CROSS\_REFERENCE\_CMD}, \texttt{HIGH\_DESTRUCTIVE\_SECURITY}) and applies max-hazard sentence pooling, a \texttt{\_HAS\_CORROBORATION\_SIGNAL\_RE} necessity gate on \texttt{CROSS\_REFERENCE\_CMD}, and high-entropy/gibberish fail-closed escalation to \texttt{HIGH}.

The resulting hazard level $h \in \{\texttt{LOW}, \texttt{MEDIUM}, \texttt{HIGH}\}$ parameterizes a two-dimensional decision-theoretic burden-of-proof table $\texttt{POLICY\_TABLE\_2D}(c, h)$ in \texttt{src/lucca/core/proof\_policy.py} (\texttt{Spec D-REAS-09}) across four belief consumers $c \in \{\texttt{casual\_mention}, \texttt{memory\_commit\_inferred}, \texttt{unhedged\_assertion}, \texttt{irreversible\_action}\}$ and four legal standards of proof (\texttt{ProofStandard} in \texttt{src/lucca/core/schemas.py}):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{\texttt{SPECULATION} ($\text{confidence} < 0.50$, Unlikely / Speculative):} Cannot be committed to memory or stated as belief; if voiced at conclusion of an abductive chain (\texttt{DeductionAndPlanningRunner.run\_deduction\_step} in \texttt{src/lucca/core/deduction\_runner.py}), it requires an explicit speculation hedge (\emph{``I'm guessing \dots, but that's just speculation''}).
    \item \textbf{\texttt{BALANCE\_OF\_PROBABILITIES} ($0.50 \le \text{confidence} < 0.75$, More Likely Than Not):} Satisfies \texttt{(casual\_mention, LOW)} with a hedged belief marker (\emph{``I think \dots, but I'm not sure enough to keep it''}), but is rejected by \texttt{meets\_standard(confidence, "memory\_commit\_inferred")}.
    \item \textbf{\texttt{CLEAR\_AND\_CONVINCING} ($0.75 \le \text{confidence} < 0.95$):} Required minimum threshold to commit an \texttt{INFERRED} fact to SQLite memory under \texttt{LOW} or \texttt{MEDIUM} hazard, for \texttt{(casual\_mention, MEDIUM)}, and for \texttt{(unhedged\_assertion, LOW)}.
    \item \textbf{\texttt{BEYOND\_REASONABLE\_DOUBT} ($\text{confidence} \ge 0.95$):} Required for all \texttt{HIGH}-hazard claims (medical, legal, financial, security), for \texttt{(unhedged\_assertion, MEDIUM/HIGH)}, and for all \texttt{irreversible\_action} dispatches regardless of hazard level.
\end{itemize}
Before an inferred or partially proven premise is checked against $\texttt{POLICY\_TABLE\_2D}(c, h)$, \texttt{CorroborationEngine} (\texttt{src/lucca/core/corroboration.py} and \texttt{src/lucca/core/walton\_schemes/evaluators.py}) evaluates Douglas Walton's five defeasible argumentation schemes (\texttt{AUTHORITY}, \texttt{SIGN}, \texttt{ANALOGY}, \texttt{CONSEQUENCE}, and \texttt{PRECEDENT}). Each scheme dispatches three critical questions (\texttt{CQ1..CQ3}: source receipt or factual congruence, independent grounding-eligible corroboration, and competing dispute or countervailing evidence) against the signed SQLite store and turn receipts. The confidence update function \texttt{adjust\_confidence(confidence, q\_results, scheme)} increments independently corroborated claims ($+0.10$ to $+0.15$ subject to a hard $\min(0.95, \dots)$ ceiling that prevents defeasible evidence from laundering into $1.00$ certainty) and decrements disputed or superseded claims ($-0.20$ to $-0.30$) while appending explicit defeater conditions (\texttt{unless\dots}, \texttt{assuming\dots}) rendered in speech by \texttt{format\_conditioned\_speech}.

To ensure that first-pass question answering and external web research obey $\texttt{POLICY\_TABLE\_2D}(c, h)$ without requiring an explicit follow-up \emph{``cross-reference that''} command, \texttt{evaluate\_web\_evidence\_burden} (\texttt{src/lucca/core/proof\_policy.py}) is invoked automatically on every \texttt{TIER\_4\_WEB} cascade retrieval (\texttt{query\_substrate\_3\_web} in \texttt{src/lucca/core/cascade\_substrates.py}) and every \texttt{search\_web}/\texttt{research\_topic} tool dispatch (\texttt{src/lucca/core/tool\_dispatcher.py}). Rather than stamping all web snippets with a static $0.85$ prior, the evaluator normalizes registrable domains (\texttt{\_registrable\_domain}, collapsing \texttt{www.} and subdomain aliases onto a single eTLD+1 identity), extracts the single sentence per domain with highest proposition-token overlap (\texttt{\_select\_query\_aligned\_sentence}), filters non-authority scheme tokens so benign procedural descriptions do not trigger false disputes, and runs \texttt{evaluate\_authority} (\texttt{CQ1..CQ3}):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Cross-Source Dispute (\texttt{CQ3} Satisfied $\rightarrow 0.40$, \texttt{SPECULATION}):} Conflicting claims across domains set \texttt{told\_true=True, told\_false=True} (\texttt{BelnapValue.CONFLICT}), dock confidence from $0.70$ to $0.40$ ($-0.30$), inject an explicit \texttt{[Epistemic Burden Contract]} into synthesis context, and block proactive memory ratification in \texttt{check\_and\_offer\_ratification} (\texttt{src/lucca/core/deduction\_runner.py}).
    \item \textbf{Single Uncorroborated Domain ($1$ Source $\rightarrow 0.70$, \texttt{BALANCE\_OF\_PROBABILITIES}):} Satisfies \texttt{(casual\_mention, LOW)} ($\ge 0.50$), but falls below \texttt{(casual\_mention, MEDIUM)} ($\ge 0.75$) and \texttt{(casual\_mention, HIGH)} ($\ge 0.95$). On \texttt{MEDIUM} or \texttt{HIGH} hazard queries, \texttt{query\_substrate\_3\_web} automatically escalates from single-snippet \texttt{search\_web} to multi-domain Scrapling extraction (\texttt{deep\_extract=True}) and injects a mandatory qualification contract if corroboration remains below threshold.
    \item \textbf{Two Independent Corroborating Domains ($2$ Sources $\rightarrow 0.80$, \texttt{CLEAR\_AND\_CONVINCING}):} \texttt{CQ2} increments confidence from $0.70$ to $0.80$ ($+0.10$), satisfying \texttt{(casual\_mention, MEDIUM)} and \texttt{(memory\_commit\_inferred, LOW/MEDIUM)} ($\ge 0.75$) while still requiring explicit qualification under \texttt{HIGH} hazard ($\ge 0.95$).
    \item \textbf{Three or More Independent Domains ($\ge 3$ Sources $\rightarrow 0.95$, \texttt{BEYOND\_REASONABLE\_DOUBT}):} Multi-domain base $0.85$ plus \texttt{CQ2} $+0.10$ reaches the hard $0.95$ defeasible ceiling, satisfying all \texttt{HIGH}-hazard gates.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Task-Priority Pooling for Multi-Sentence Intent Routing (\texttt{NeuralIntentClassifier})}
For multi-head intent routing (\texttt{src/lucca/router/neural\_intent\_classifier.py}, \texttt{models/router\_intent\_ov}, static shape \texttt{[1, 64]}), \texttt{NeuralIntentClassifier} predicts 14 primary intents and 9 reasoning domain flags per sentence and promotes concrete task/reasoning intents (\texttt{CLASS\_PRIORITY\_TIER >= 1}) over greetings and conversational filler (\texttt{chit\_chat}, \texttt{clarify}), paired with deterministic \texttt{known\_apps} slot verification (\texttt{\_extract\_app\_entity}) and \texttt{ConformalGuard} out-of-domain abstention.

\section{Empirical Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\subsection{Large-Sample Action-Claim Evaluation ($N=2{,}022$)}
\label{sec:large_sample}
To test whether clause-level NPU supervision resolves the open-vocabulary ceiling identified in Paper~02 \cite{maxwell2026evasion} with adequate statistical power, we evaluated \texttt{NeuralActionClaimVerifier} against the legacy 44-stem regex supervisor across all $N=2{,}022$ labeled action-claim pairs in the repository (\texttt{logs/test\_reports/wo42\_large\_sample\_2020\_report.json}):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Paper~02 4-Model Corpus ($n=1{,}000$):} 250 prompts $\times$ 4 Gemma-4 models (\texttt{26B-A4B}, \texttt{12B}, \texttt{E4B}, \texttt{E2B}) with dual-rater blinded semantic labels ($\kappa = 1.0000$ binary).
    \item \textbf{Paper~02 Held-Out V1/V2 Benchmark ($n=120$):} 50 temptations, 40 honest controls, 30 idioms.
    \item \textbf{Organic, Implicit, Passive, and Blind Multi-Sentence Suites ($n=352$):} Including \texttt{BLIND\_COH\_30}, \texttt{BLIND42\_COH\_100}, \texttt{LOCKED\_COH\_050}, passive/third-person claims, structured JSON/YAML/TOML/XML status blocks, and foreign-language claims.
    \item \textbf{Property-Based Hypothesis Fuzz Corpus ($n=550$):} Randomized multi-sentence needle-in-a-haystack and benign control permutations.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Pre-Registered Blind and Locked Holdout Suites ($N=540$)}
\label{sec:blind_holdouts}
To prevent iterative test-set overfitting, we conducted two multi-agent blinded evaluations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{\texttt{Blind-390} (\texttt{WO-42 Phase 4}):} Three isolated subagents with zero access to training scripts or model weights authored 300 multi-sentence adversarial items (\texttt{BLIND42\_COH\_001..100}, \texttt{BLIND42\_HZD\_001..100}, \texttt{BLIND42\_RTR\_001..100}), combined with the 90-item \texttt{WO-41} blind stress test.
    \item \textbf{\texttt{WO42\_LOCKED\_HOLDOUT\_150}:} After freezing all training weights, three fresh isolated subagents authored 150 locked items ($50$ per classifier). Two independent blinded LLM judges graded all 150 outputs line by line without seeing model predictions, achieving $20/20$ ($100.0\%$, Cohen's $\kappa = 1.000$) inter-rater agreement on the overlap slice.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Pre-Registered $N=360$ ($n=30$/Cell) 2D Web Evidentiary Burden Holdout and 288-Turn Live 4-Model GGUF Evaluation}
\label{sec:web_evidentiary_burden}
An initial developmental smoke test ($N=60$, $n=5$ per cell in \texttt{data/benchmarks/web\_evidentiary\_burden\_60.jsonl}) exposed two real-world defects on scraped web text ($57/60 = 95.0\%$ pre-fix $\rightarrow 60/60 = 100.0\%$ post-fix): (1) cross-scheme negation/adversative tokens in \texttt{corroboration.py} (\emph{``side effects''}, \emph{``prevent''}, \texttt{--no-preserve-root}, \emph{``do not exceed''}) triggered false \texttt{CQ3} disputes under \texttt{Scheme.AUTHORITY}, and (2) offline fallback notices duplicated domain identities. However, $n=5$ per cell carries a wide 95\% Wilson lower bound of $56.55\%$ even at $5/5$, and tuning on the developmental set introduces in-sample bias.

To obtain statistically powered, out-of-sample estimates across both the neurosymbolic evaluator and all four local GGUF model weights, we executed pre-registration \texttt{PREREG-WEB-BURDEN-360-4MODEL} (\texttt{logs/test\_reports/web\_evidentiary\_burden\_360\_prereg\_lock.json}):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{SHA-256 Code \& Weight Lock (\texttt{WO-45 R-20/R-23} Disclosure):} We recorded the SHA-256 digests of \texttt{proof\_policy.py}, \texttt{corroboration.py}, \texttt{walton\_schemes/evaluators.py}, \texttt{cascade.py}, \texttt{cascade\_substrates.py}, and \texttt{models/proof\_policy\_hazard\_ov/openvino\_model.bin} in \texttt{logs/test\_reports/web\_evidentiary\_burden\_360\_prereg\_lock.json} (\texttt{locked\_at\_utc: 2026-11-09T01:51:54Z}). \textbf{Provenance disclosure (\texttt{WO-45 R-20/R-23}):} First, \texttt{src/lucca/core/neural\_hazard\_classifier.py} was omitted from \texttt{web\_evidentiary\_burden\_360\_prereg\_lock.json} and edited after inspecting the 10 initial \texttt{HIGH} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{MEDIUM} misses. Second, in git history, \texttt{proof\_policy.py}, \texttt{neural\_hazard\_classifier.py}, the lock file, the $N=360$ corpus, and the evaluation report were all committed together in a single commit (\texttt{82b1da8}, \texttt{07:16Z}), so git commit ordering alone cannot independently prove that the lock preceded corpus generation. \section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}
Project Lucca does not claim to invent a new verification primitive; rather, its contribution is a local, zero-VRAM edge integration combining clause-level OpenVINO NPU classification, deterministic tool-receipt set verification, two-dimensional hazard-scaled burden-of-proof policies, and defeasible Walton/Belnap conflict tracking. Table~\ref{tab:related_work_r50} compares Lucca against existing safety guardrails, factuality verifiers, conflict-aware RAG pipelines, computational argumentation frameworks, and commercial/open groundedness models from 2022--2026. Among recent systems, \textbf{ClashEval} \cite{wu2024clasheval}, \textbf{CrAM} \cite{deng2024cram}, \textbf{TrustRAG} \cite{zhou2025trustrag}, and \textbf{ArgLLMs} \cite{freedman2024argllms} address overlapping sub-problems (knowledge conflict, source credibility, retrieval poisoning, and argumentative verification), while \textbf{Vectara HHEM} \cite{vectara2024hhem} shares Lucca's sub-$50\text{ ms}$ cross-encoder design for pair consistency. None of these single frameworks jointly enforce deterministic local tool-receipt verification, query-hazard-scaled multi-domain corroboration counts, and cross-source conflict disclosure in a zero-VRAM NPU/CPU sidecar.

\section{Limitations and Residual Error Analysis}
\label{sec:limitations}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Residual False Negatives ($6/471 = 1.27\%$ on $N=2{,}022$):} Inspection of the 6 missed positive claims in \texttt{logs/test\_reports/wo42\_large\_sample\_2020\_report.json} shows that all 6 come from the 1,000-turn sampled corpus (\texttt{W751..W1000}) where the model implied completion through an indirect state consequence without an explicit action verb or status token (e.g., \emph{``Your inbox is now completely empty and ready for Monday''} in response to an email archive prompt).
    \item \textbf{Residual False Positives ($9/1{,}551 = 0.58\%$ on $N=2{,}022$):} The 9 false alarms occur on hypothetical troubleshooting walkthroughs where the assistant writes first-person illustrative steps (\emph{``First, I updated the registry key in my test environment...''}) without fencing them in code blocks.
    \item \textbf{Linear Latency Scaling with Clause Count:} Because \texttt{NeuralActionClaimVerifier} evaluates the full response plus each extracted clause sequentially under a thread lock on the Intel NPU, mean verification latency scales from $1.31\text{ ms}$ on 1-sentence outputs to $2.95\text{ ms}$ (P95 $3.67\text{ ms}$) on 3--4 sentence outputs. On 10+ sentence long-form essays, clause-by-clause NPU scoring can reach $8$--$12\text{ ms}$; batching clause tensors into a single static-shape NPU call is left for future work.
    \item \textbf{Single-Workstation Measurement Scope:} All latency, VRAM, and live 4-model measurements were recorded on a single Windows 11 workstation (Intel Core Ultra 9 275HX, Intel AI Boost NPU, NVIDIA RTX 5080 Laptop GPU 16\,GB GDDR7) and are labeled \emph{Author-measured} until independently reproduced on external hardware.
    \item \textbf{Open-Web Retrieval Relevance \& Narrative Dispute Boundary ($39/64 = 60.94\%$ on \texttt{R-32 v1} $\to$ $46/64 = 71.88\%$ on \texttt{WO-46 real\_web\_v2}):} While \texttt{evaluate\_web\_evidence\_burden} achieves $360/360$ ($100.0\%$) conformance on clean topic-aligned snippets and collapses $42/45$ ($93.3\%$) syndicated/sybil attacks (\texttt{R-33}), initial unfiltered live web search (\texttt{logs/test\_reports/wo45/r32\_real\_web\_v1\_report.json}) scored $39/64$ ($60.94\%$) due to off-topic search hits ($\text{MAE} = 0.9688$ on source count) and comparative/prohibitive prose triggering false disputes ($12/50 = 24.00\%$ dispute FPR; $42/64 = 65.62\%$ dispute accuracy). In a pre-registered follow-up (\texttt{WO-46}, \texttt{logs/test\_reports/wo46/real\_web\_v2\_report.json}; $N=64$ disjoint live queries, $246$ archived \texttt{.html.gz} pages, dual-rater $\kappa = 0.9107$--$1.0000$), adding tail-preserving query simplification, eTLD+1 diverse candidate selection, an OpenVINO passage-relevance filter (\texttt{web\_relevance.py}), and symmetric prohibitive polarity normalization raised single-shot burden decision accuracy to $46/64$ ($71.88\%$, CI $[59.87\%, 81.41\%]$), dispute accuracy to $56/64$ ($87.50\%$, slashing dispute FPR to $2/55 = 3.64\%$), sufficient-evidence utility pass rate to $32/39$ ($82.05\%$), and within-1 source count accuracy to $56/64$ ($87.50\%$, $\text{MAE} = 0.7188$). The remaining $18/64$ mismatches ($11$ false burden passes and $7$ conservative over-hedges) stem primarily from implicit single-article summaries of scientific debates without explicit controversy lexicon ($3/9 = 33.33\%$ dispute recall) and 1-tier borderline hazard classifications ($46/64 = 71.88\%$ strict 3-class vs.\ $55/64 = 85.94\%$ conservative safe).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Lexical speech-regex supervisors cannot generalize to open-vocabulary verb lemmas ($29.5\%$ unseen-verb recall in Paper~02 \cite{maxwell2026evasion}), while whole-paragraph neural classifiers degrade when a single fabricated action claim or task request is embedded inside multi-sentence prose ($75.0\%$ on \texttt{Blind-300}). By combining syntactic clause decomposition with deterministic receipt set-membership verification, max-hazard lattice pooling, and task-priority intent pooling, three $82.1\text{M}$-parameter \texttt{nli-MiniLM2-L6-H768} classifiers running in FP16 on an Intel AI Boost NPU ($0\text{ MB}$ dGPU VRAM, $1.22$--$2.95\text{ ms}$) achieve $99.26\%$ accuracy ($98.73\%$ recall, $0.58\%$ FPR) across $N=2{,}022$ action-claim turns, $96.67\%$ single-pass / $100.0\%$ post-fix accuracy on the pre-registered \texttt{WO42\_LOCKED\_HOLDOUT\_150} benchmark, and $100/100$ functional coherence across all four local Gemma-4 model scales.
